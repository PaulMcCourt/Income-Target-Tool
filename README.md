

# 💰 How Much to Live Where — Income Target Tool

## 📌 Project Overview

This tool answers a simple but important question:

> *"How much do I need to earn to live where I want, given my family needs to maintain a modest lifestyle?"*

Users can explore required income across the United States by filtering on:

- 📍 **Geographic location** — State and County
- 👨‍👩‍👧 **Household structure** — Number of adults and children
- 📅 **Timeframe** — Annual or monthly expense view

The dashboard empowers users to compare required income, expense distributions, and cost of living across different states and counties side by side.

## 📊 Executive Summary

Where you live and how many people you support are two of the most 
consequential financial variables a household faces — yet most salary 
conversations ignore both.

This tool draws on the EPI's 2025 Family Budget Calculator to make 
those variables tangible and comparable. A family of four in Mississippi 
needs roughly $84,000 to cover modest living costs. The same family in 
Washington D.C. needs nearly $156,000 — an $71,000 gap for an identical 
lifestyle. Each additional child adds around $16,000–$21,000 to 
annual costs nationally, with single-parent households facing 
disproportionately higher burdens relative to income.

Rather than presenting a single conclusion, this dashboard is designed 
for exploration — allowing job seekers, families considering relocation, 
and anyone negotiating a salary to interrogate the data on their own terms.

## 💡 Recommendations

This tool is designed to inform decisions, not make them. Used thoughtfully, 
it can meaningfully sharpen salary negotiations, relocation planning, and 
household budgeting. The following guidance will help you get the most out 
of it.

### Using the Tool Effectively

**Use it as a floor, not a ceiling.** The EPI Family Budget Calculator is 
designed around a *modest* standard of living — covering necessities without 
much discretionary spending. If you want to save aggressively, service debt, 
or maintain a more comfortable lifestyle, treat the figures as a minimum 
starting point and adjust upward accordingly.

**Pair it with salary data.** The tool tells you what you *need* to earn — 
it doesn't tell you what you *can* earn in a given location. Cross-reference 
the required income figures with tools like the Bureau of Labor Statistics, 
Glassdoor, or Levels.fyi to understand whether the local job market can 
actually support that income in your field.

**Think beyond the state level.** Cost of living can vary enormously within 
a single state. A required income in rural Tennessee looks very different from 
Nashville. Use the county-level filters in the dashboard to get as granular 
as your situation demands.

**Model your actual household.** The dashboard supports a range of family 
structures from a single adult to two adults with four children. Run your 
specific household configuration rather than a generic benchmark — the 
difference of even one child can add $16,000–$21,000 to your annual 
required income.

---

### Limitations to Keep in Mind

**The data reflects 2025 conditions.** Cost of living, housing markets, and 
tax structures change year to year. Treat the figures as a strong directional 
guide rather than a precise forecast, and revisit them if making decisions 
more than a year from now.

**Childcare costs vary significantly by availability.** The EPI dataset 
includes childcare as a cost category, but local availability and subsidy 
programs can dramatically affect what families actually pay. In some counties, 
actual childcare costs may be higher or lower than the modelled figure.

**Taxes are estimated, not exact.** The tax figures are modelled estimates 
based on average effective rates and do not account for individual 
circumstances such as deductions, credits, investment income, or 
employer benefits.

**The dataset does not include all geographies.** Some rural counties have 
limited or no data. If your target location returns no results, the nearest 
metro area is a reasonable proxy.

## 📁 Data Source

Based on the **Economic Policy Institute (EPI) Family Budget Calculator**, which provides cost of living estimates across U.S. metro and non-metro areas for families of different sizes.

🔗 [EPI Family Budget Dataset](https://www.epi.org/resources/budget/)
